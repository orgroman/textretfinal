# State-of-the-Art Retrieval Methods on TREC Robust04

**TREC Robust04** (Robust 2004) is a standard benchmark for ad-hoc document retrieval, featuring 249 topics over ~528K news articles[\[1\]](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.17082v2#:~:text=TREC%20Robust04%20,Huston%20and%20Croft%2C%20%2082). Mean Average Precision (MAP) on Robust04 (using short "title" queries by default) has long been a key metric to gauge IR progress. Below we survey **recent cutting-edge retrieval methods** - both **neural and traditional** - that have achieved state-of-the-art (SOTA) MAP on Robust04 or show strong potential to do so. We organize methods by their approach (sparse lexical, dense, hybrid, reranking, etc.), report their **year, MAP on Robust04**, approach type, key techniques, and provide references. A comparative **leaderboard** is also included.

The current **state of the art** on Robust04 _title_ queries is around **0.38-0.40 MAP**, achieved by multi-stage pipelines that combine neural rerankers with feedback expansion. Notably, Gao & Callan's **MORES+ (2022)** set a new SOTA on _description_ queries (MAP ≈0.438)[\[3\]](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.17082v2#:~:text=NDCG%20MAP%20R%401000%20NDCG%20MAP,377), and around 0.39 on titles - edging out the earlier MonoT5-3B reranker (0.384)[\[2\]](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.06713#:~:text=%2B%20T5,to%20run%20these%20experiments%20due). Below, we detail each category of method.

## Traditional Lexical Methods (Sparse Retrieval)

**Okapi BM25** (Robertson et al., 1994) is the classic bag-of-words ranking function. On Robust04's news corpus, BM25 yields about **MAP 0.25** as a first-stage ranker[\[2\]](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.06713#:~:text=%2B%20T5,to%20run%20these%20experiments%20due). Variants like **BM25+RM3** pseudo-relevance feedback (RM3, circa 2004) improve this to **~0.29 MAP**[\[2\]](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.06713#:~:text=%2B%20T5,to%20run%20these%20experiments%20due) by expanding the query with top-ranked terms. These non-neural methods were strong baselines up to the late 2010s.

- _Key technique:_ BM25 uses term-frequency and document frequency weighting (TF-IDF style) with document length normalization[\[16\]](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-82871-0?error=cookies_not_supported&code=39586133-7e4d-4b78-a9a5-0fb6cd5f5a6b#:~:text=term%20in%20the%20document%20in,the%20RM1%20and%20RM2%20models). RM3 feedback then adds high-probability terms from the top-retrieved documents back into the query[\[16\]](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-82871-0?error=cookies_not_supported&code=39586133-7e4d-4b78-a9a5-0fb6cd5f5a6b#:~:text=term%20in%20the%20document%20in,the%20RM1%20and%20RM2%20models), addressing vocabulary mismatch.

Despite their simplicity, lexical methods remain competitive for short queries. For instance, Gao & Callan note that for one-word **title queries**, "lighter" methods like BM25 (with feedback) can perform nearly on par with complex neural models[\[15\]](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2205.04275#:~:text=On%20Title%20queries%2C%20many%20neural,compared%20with%20MORES%2B%20entries%2C%20the)[\[17\]](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2205.04275#:~:text=our%20experiments%2C%20we%20see%20on,use%20the%20same%20initial%20retriever). However, on longer **description queries** (more natural language), lexical approaches struggle to capture meaning, and neural methods pull ahead[\[18\]](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2205.04275#:~:text=On%20the%20other%20hand%2C%20when,will%20have%20further%20future%20impacts).

**Axiomatic and Expansion Variants:** Other pre-neural advances included _axiomatic retrieval functions_ (e.g. F2-EXP) and cluster-based or ensemble approaches. In fact, prior to BERT, the **best Robust04 MAP (~0.3686)** was achieved by a non-neural **ensemble of strong lexical runs**[\[6\]](https://aclanthology.org/anthology-files/pdf/D/D19/D19-3004.pdf#:~:text=known%20AP%20reported%20on%20the,neural%20method%20based%20on%20ensembles). This stood as the "number to beat" until neural rankers emerged around 2019.

## Learned Sparse Models (Lexical-Neural Hybrids)

**Learned sparse retrieval** methods use neural networks to produce **lexical term-weighted representations**, bridging the gap between traditional IR and deep learning:

- **Doc2query (Nogueira et al., 2019):** This method uses a T5 transformer to _generate probable queries for each document_ (e.g. predicting questions the document could answer)[\[19\]](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-82871-0?error=cookies_not_supported&code=39586133-7e4d-4b78-a9a5-0fb6cd5f5a6b#:~:text=information%20retrieval%2C%20particularly%20for%20improving,dimensional%20semantic%20information%20from). These synthetic queries are indexed alongside the document text. The effect is increased recall - documents become "expanded" with new terms. DocT5query improved Robust04 retrieval by adding 10-20% more relevant terms, yielding MAP around **0.28-0.30** (a boost over BM25 ~0.253) in evaluations[\[14\]](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.06713#:~:text=Model%20arxiv,2). _Technique:_ T5 seq2seq model is fine-tuned to generate questions from passages; top-K generated queries per doc are indexed.
- **DeepCT (Dai & Callan, 2019):** Rather than adding terms, DeepCT learns to **re-weight terms** in each document using a BERT-based regression model. It assigns higher weights to terms important for relevance (e.g., distinguishing "river _bank_" vs "financial _bank_"). These weights modify term frequencies in the index. On Robust04, DeepCT likewise raised MAP into the high 0.20s (approx 0.28) range (significantly above BM25) in cross-validation experiments[\[15\]](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2205.04275#:~:text=On%20Title%20queries%2C%20many%20neural,compared%20with%20MORES%2B%20entries%2C%20the). _Technique:_ The model predicts a "term importance" score for each term in context[\[15\]](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2205.04275#:~:text=On%20Title%20queries%2C%20many%20neural,compared%20with%20MORES%2B%20entries%2C%20the), which then scales that term's frequency in the indexing process.
- **SPLADE & SPLADE++ (Formal et al., 2021-22):** **SPLADE** ("Sparse Lexical and Expansion model") uses a BERT-based encoder to produce very high-dimensional sparse vectors for queries and documents[\[20\]](https://ciir-publications.cs.umass.edu/getpdf.php?id=1508#:~:text=publications,BERT%2C%20to%20learn%20sparse). Essentially, the model learns to activate relevant terms from the vocabulary for each document (including implicit expansions), with a penalty to keep representations sparse. SPLADE can be used as a first-stage retriever via an inverted index of these learned term weights. It _largely outperforms BM25 on datasets like Robust04_[\[21\]](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3634912#:~:text=Retrieval%20dl,report%20in%20Table%209) - in zero-shot evaluations, SPLADE trained on MS MARCO achieved **~0.30-0.32 MAP** on Robust04 titles, beating BM25 by ~20%[\[10\]](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.17082v2#:~:text=NDCG%20MAP%20R%401000%20NDCG%20MAP,). Later distilled versions (**SPLADE v2 / SPLADE++**) further improved efficiency and effectiveness, becoming a state-of-the-art learned sparse retriever by 2022. _Key techniques:_ iterative contrastive learning, document expansion through the model itself, and TF-IDF regularization to limit the number of active terms[\[20\]](https://ciir-publications.cs.umass.edu/getpdf.php?id=1508#:~:text=publications,BERT%2C%20to%20learn%20sparse).
- **UniCOIL (Lin et al., 2021):** This is another learned sparse approach where a dual-encoder (like a distilled BERT) assigns a **single weight per term** (unlike SPLADE's many expansions). It was an outcome of knowledge distillation from ColBERT to a simpler representation. UniCOIL has lower recall than SPLADE but is simpler. In practice, UniCOIL+ (with doc expansion) achieved Robust04 MAP in the low 0.30s, on par with SPLADE's range (not a new record, but strong for first-stage retrieval).

_Why these matter:_ Learned sparse models retain compatibility with inverted indexes, making them efficient and interpretable (we can see which terms are activated). They often serve as powerful _initial retrievers_, which can then be reranked by expensive models. For example, Formal et al. show a BM25+RM3+Neural reranker pipeline got 0.377 MAP on Robust04 titles, while **SPLADE+Neural reranker** was competitive at 0.309 MAP (zero-shot)[\[10\]](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.17082v2#:~:text=NDCG%20MAP%20R%401000%20NDCG%20MAP,) - and with domain adaptation, SPLADE would further improve. These methods effectively bring semantic recall improvements while still leveraging lexical matching.

## Dense Vector Retrievers (Dual Encoders)

Dense retrievers use learned **embeddings** instead of explicit terms. A dual-encoder neural network encodes queries and documents into vectors, and relevant docs are found via nearest-neighbor search in the embedding space (often with inner product similarity). Dense methods became prominent with advancements in representation learning:

- **DPR - Dense Passage Retriever (Karpukhin et al., 2020):** Although developed for QA, DPR's approach (two BERT encoders for questions and passages) inspired many IR models. DPR was tuned on QA data, so direct Robust04 results aren't reported, but it paved the way.
- **ANCE (Xiong et al., 2020):** **ANCE** introduced _asynchronous negative training_ - iteratively mining hard negatives using the model itself. ANCE trained on MS MARCO (a large web corpus) in a **zero-shot** setting yields Robust04 MAP around **0.30** (significantly better than chance but slightly below BM25)[\[13\]](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0306457322001364#:~:text=expansion%20www,the%20Robust04%20and%20WT10G). With some adaptation or hybridization, ANCE can exceed BM25 - e.g. Macdonald et al. (2021) found ColBERT or ANCE used for _external expansion_ can boost robust04 effectiveness[\[22\]](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0306457322001364#:~:text=). Generally, **dense bi-encoders alone struggled to fully beat strong lexical baselines on Robust04** without additional tricks, likely due to domain shift (news vs. training domain) and the importance of rare term matches.
- **ColBERT (Khattab et al., 2020):** **ColBERT** is a _late-interaction_ model - a hybrid between dense and exact matching. It encodes each token in a document into a vector; a query vector only matches if it has a high dot-product with _some_ token vector of the doc (MaxSim). This preserves some aspect of term-level matching in the dense space. ColBERT _fine-tuned on MS MARCO_ was highly effective on passage ranking and **excelled on Robust04** when combined with reranking or feedback[\[23\]](https://aclanthology.org/anthology-files/pdf/D/D19/D19-3004.pdf#:~:text=Researchers%20have%20developed%20a%20panoply,which%20in%02tegrates%20the%20Anserini%20information). As a first-stage retriever, ColBERT (zero-shot) roughly matches BM25 on Robust04 (mid-0.20s MAP)[\[13\]](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0306457322001364#:~:text=expansion%20www,the%20Robust04%20and%20WT10G), but when fine-tuned or combined with feedback it shines. A **White Box analysis** noted ColBERT-type models "have excelled where previous neural models faltered - e.g. on Robust04"[\[24\]](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/347441696_A_White_Box_Analysis_of_ColBERT#:~:text=,representations%20onto%20the%20target), thanks to modeling term contexts.
- **ColBERT v2 (Santhanam et al., 2021):** This improved version compresses document representations (using residual compression and better late interaction scoring). ColBERTv2 improved both efficiency and effectiveness slightly; with fine-tuning, it can surpass the original. While exact robust04 numbers aren't widely published, it's expected to outperform ColBERT v1 by a small margin.
- **TCT-ColBERT (Lin et al., 2020):** Instead of using ColBERT at runtime, Lin et al. distilled ColBERT's interaction knowledge into a _dual encoder_. Their **TCT-ColBERT** model produces a single vector per document (like ANCE) but retains some of ColBERT's gains via training on soft labels from ColBERT[\[11\]](https://huggingface.co/papers/2010.11386#:~:text=Distilling%20Dense%20Representations%20for%20Ranking,Chieh%20Lin). TCT-ColBERT was shown to outperform vanilla DPR/ANCE on certain benchmarks. On Robust04, it likely scores around **MAP 0.30** as well (castorini's replication reports TCT-ColBERT is on par or slightly above BM25 in that range).
- **Condenser / coCondenser (Gao & Callan 2021):** These are not retrieval models per se, but **pre-training techniques** that produce better dense encoders. coCondenser pre-trained BERT with an IR objective and yielded strong results on BEIR (including robust04). For example, a _coCondenser-based bi-encoder_ combined with MORES+ (see below) was used as the initial retriever for MS MARCO and achieved top scores[\[25\]](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2205.04275#:~:text=MS,493). In essence, better pre-training leads to better dense representations for zero-shot retrieval.

**Summary:** Dense retrievers offer semantic generalization - finding relevant documents even if exact words don't match. However, on Robust04's news articles (with relatively formal language and moderate topic diversity), dense models alone did not drastically surpass lexical methods until hybrid and feedback methods were applied. Dense vectors often missed rare term matches that were obvious to BM25. Thus, many SOTA pipelines use _dense+lexical hybrids_ or use dense retrieval as a recall booster combined with rerankers.

## Neural Rerankers (Cross-Encoders and Sequence-to-Sequence)

Neural rerankers are models (typically Transformers) that take a query and a candidate document and output a relevance score. They are applied on top of an initial retrieval (e.g. top 100 from BM25) to re-score and sort those candidates. These have driven large improvements in Robust04 MAP:

- **MonoBERT (Nogueira & Cho, 2019):** This was a BERT _cross-encoder_ fine-tuned on MS MARCO for passage ranking[\[26\]](https://aclanthology.org/anthology-files/pdf/D/D19/D19-3004.pdf#:~:text=previous%20approaches%20to%20document%20retrieval,which%20in%02tegrates%20the%20Anserini%20information). MonoBERT reads the full query and document text together and predicts relevance (classification). Applied to Robust04 (usually by fine-tuning on a few Robust topics or zero-shot from MS MARCO), MonoBERT significantly improves over BM25. For example, a BM25 baseline ~0.253 MAP jumps to **~0.37 MAP** with a BERT-large reranker[\[9\]](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.06713#:~:text=our%20model%20with%20CEDR%20,3B%20model%20produces%20the). Yilmaz et al. (Birch) reported BERT-based reranking exceeded the previous 0.3686 record, reaching **MAP 0.369**[\[6\]](https://aclanthology.org/anthology-files/pdf/D/D19/D19-3004.pdf#:~:text=known%20AP%20reported%20on%20the,neural%20method%20based%20on%20ensembles). Nogueira et al. similarly noted BM25+MARCO-BERT gave ~0.372 MAP[\[9\]](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.06713#:~:text=our%20model%20with%20CEDR%20,3B%20model%20produces%20the). _Technique:_ 12-layer Transformer encoding \[CLS\] Query + Document, using \[CLS\] score for ranking.
- **Birch (Yilmaz et al., EMNLP 2019):** Birch integrated BERT into Anserini for end-to-end document search[\[27\]](https://aclanthology.org/anthology-files/pdf/D/D19/D19-3004.pdf#:~:text=Abstract%20We%20present%20Birch%2C%20a,and%20social%20media%20test%20collections). It fine-tuned on MS MARCO, then on Robust04 (and other collections) via multi-stage fine-tuning. Birch achieved **MAP ~0.37** and was one of the first to demonstrate **state-of-the-art on Robust04 with BERT**[\[6\]](https://aclanthology.org/anthology-files/pdf/D/D19/D19-3004.pdf#:~:text=known%20AP%20reported%20on%20the,neural%20method%20based%20on%20ensembles). Notably, it _beat the best non-neural ensemble_ and showed BERT's effectiveness even on long documents. Birch's success also highlighted engineering challenges (managing BERT inference over large collections) that inspired subsequent work.
- **CEDR (MacAvaney et al., 2019):** CEDR combined BERT with classic IR features by integrating BERT-based representations into neural ranking architectures (like PACRR/KNRM). CEDR was trained on Robust04 via 5-fold cross-validation. While effective (it improved over prior KNRM/etc.), it did not exceed Birch's performance. For context, CEDR's cross-val MAP was around 0.34 (and a variant ensemble hit 0.368, equal to the old SOTA)[\[6\]](https://aclanthology.org/anthology-files/pdf/D/D19/D19-3004.pdf#:~:text=known%20AP%20reported%20on%20the,neural%20method%20based%20on%20ensembles). The lesson was that **pre-training and fine-tuning on large data (MS MARCO)** gave Birch an edge, whereas CEDR trained only on the limited Robust data.
- **MonoT5 (Nogueira et al., 2020):** Instead of BERT, Nogueira et al. explored a T5 _sequence-to-sequence_ model for ranking[\[28\]](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.06713#:~:text=Results%20on%20Robust04%20are%20shown,3B%20indicate%20that%20those%20results). **MonoT5** generates a token (e.g. "true" or "false") to indicate relevance. The largest version, MonoT5-3B, set a new record on Robust04 **without any robust-specific training**: **MAP 0.384** zero-shot (BM25+RM3 → MonoT5-3B)[\[2\]](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.06713#:~:text=%2B%20T5,to%20run%20these%20experiments%20due). This was _to the authors' knowledge the highest known Robust04 MAP at the time_[\[29\]](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.06713#:~:text=match%20at%20L295%20the%20MS,produces%20the%20highest%20known%20scores). T5's text-to-text formulation and massive parameters likely better captured semantics. Interestingly, MonoT5 improved even the strong BM25+RM3 baseline (0.29→0.384)[\[2\]](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.06713#:~:text=%2B%20T5,to%20run%20these%20experiments%20due). _Key techniques:_ sequence-to-sequence training for relevance (providing more training signal per example), very large model (3 billion parameters) with knowledge from unsupervised pre-training.
- **DistilBERT and Lightweight Rerankers:** Because BERT-base/large are slow, researchers created distilled or smaller models. **DistilBERT** (Sanh et al., 2019) has 40% fewer parameters than BERT-base. When fine-tuned for ranking, DistilBERT achieves ~95% of BERT's effectiveness at ~2× speed. For example, _DistilBERT reranker outperforms a Query Likelihood baseline on Robust04_ comfortably[\[30\]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7148226/#:~:text=Central%20pmc,QL%29%20model), and is only slightly behind full BERT. Another line, **TAS-B** (Hofstätter et al., 2020), used teacher-student distillation and balanced topic sampling to train MiniLM and DistilBERT as efficient rankers. These distilled models likely reach **MAP ~0.34-0.35** on Robust04 (a bit below 0.37 of BERT) while being much faster. _Technique:_ knowledge distillation from a high-capacity cross-encoder to a smaller one (e.g., using soft labels or intermediate layer alignment).
- **Large Language Model (LLM) Rerankers (2022-2025):** Very recently, researchers have used LLMs (like GPT-3/4 or Flan-T5 XXL) to perform ranking **without traditional fine-tuning**, by prompting them. For instance, Wang et al. (2023) show that instruction-tuned LLMs can do zero-shot reranking by processing the query and document text in a prompt and outputting a relevance score or label[\[31\]](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-emnlp.305.pdf#:~:text=Large%20language%20models%20,accuracy%20in%20tasks%20like%20question). These **prompt-based rerankers** are surprisingly strong: one study found a prompt-based Flan-T5-XXL (11B parameters) achieved **MAP in the mid-0.40s on Robust04** - roughly **0.45** - for description queries[\[32\]](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-emnlp.305.pdf#:~:text=FLAN,26). This is on par with the best supervised models like MORES+. Another approach, **RankGPT (Ma et al., 2024)**, uses GPT-4 in a _listwise_ tournament style to compare documents; it achieved very high NDCG@10 (around 0.65 on Robust desc.)[\[33\]](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-emnlp.305.pdf#:~:text=Flan,4), though its exact MAP is not reported (likely also ~0.44-0.46). These methods leverage the vast knowledge and reasoning ability of LLMs. _Techniques:_ Few-shot prompts (e.g., giving examples of relevant vs non-relevant), pairwise comparisons in natural language, and chain-of-thought justifications to aid the LLM's decision. The downside is high computational cost and some instability, but they represent a new frontier when large models are accessible.

**In summary**, cross-encoder rerankers (BERT, T5, etc.) provide the biggest single-model improvements on Robust04, often boosting MAP by 50%+ over BM25[\[2\]](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.06713#:~:text=%2B%20T5,to%20run%20these%20experiments%20due). The best results come from _combining_ these rerankers with strong initial retrieval and possibly expansion, as we discuss next.

## Pseudo-Relevance Feedback & Multi-Stage Retrieval

Combining multiple retrieval stages often yields the highest performance on Robust04. Classic **pseudo-relevance feedback (PRF)** - exemplified by RM3 - has been augmented with neural models in various ways:

- **BERT-QE and CEQE (2020):** These methods perform **Contextualized Query Expansion** using BERT. For example, **CEQE** (Clinical Embeddings for Query Expansion, 2020) takes the top documents and uses BERT embeddings to select expansion terms. CEQE was reported to outperform static expansion by up to _18% on Robust04_[\[34\]](https://maroo.cs.umass.edu/getpdf.php?id=1390#:~:text=Expansion%20maroo,Deep%20Learning%20on%20average). One variant, _CEQE-MaxPool_, was noted in 2024 to still outperform some newer PRF methods[\[7\]](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-82871-0?error=cookies_not_supported&code=39586133-7e4d-4b78-a9a5-0fb6cd5f5a6b#:~:text=match%20at%20L873%20advanced%20models,to%20the%20CEQE%20model%E2%80%99s%20effective). The approach demonstrates that letting BERT read feedback documents can pick better expansion terms than frequency alone. **BERT-QE** (Zheng et al., 2020) similarly showed significant gains over a BERT-only ranker by using an unsupervised chunk-level expansion from the feedback set[\[35\]](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1016/j.ipm.2021.102672#:~:text=Contextualized%20query%20expansion%20via%20unsupervised,QE%20consists%20of%20three) - effectively marrying BERT's semantic strength with PRF.
- **ColBERT-PRF (Wang et al., 2021):** This is a _dense retrieval_ spin on feedback. ColBERT-PRF performs an initial dense retrieval, then expands the query by using the _centroid of top document embeddings_ or by mining salient terms/vectors from top docs[\[36\]](https://dl.acm.org/doi/full/10.1145/3572405#:~:text=,This%20suggests). In **zero-shot** evaluations, ColBERT-PRF was **highly effective on Robust04**, achieving performance on par with TREC Deep Learning 2019 best systems[\[37\]](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0306457322001364#:~:text=Improving%20zero,TREC%202019%20Deep%20Learning). In fact, **feedback with ColBERT gave up to +21% MAP improvement** over ColBERT alone on Robust04[\[8\]](https://discovery.researcher.life/topic/pseudo-relevance-feedback-technique/24929746?page=1&topic_name=Pseudo%20Relevance%20Feedback%20Technique#:~:text=Pseudo%20Relevance%20Feedback%20Technique%20Research,on%20the%20MSMARCO%20document). This put ColBERT-PRF's MAP around the low 0.30s (since ColBERT alone was ~0.25, +21% ≈0.30). The authors note this verifies that even dense retrievers benefit from feedback, especially in out-of-domain cases[\[38\]](https://www.bohrium.com/paper-details/what-else-would-i-like-a-user-simulator-using-alternatives-for-improved-evaluation-of-fashion-conversational-recommendation-systems/966035952621322308-108611#:~:text=).
- **GAR (Explicit Entity Expansion, 2022):** Some methods like GAR (Graph Augmented Retrieval) incorporate external knowledge - e.g. expanding queries with related _entities_. Notably, **GAR-ENT** (which adds linked knowledge-base entities) and **GAR-ColBERT** (which adds dense expansion) were explored on Robust04[\[39\]](https://keirworkshop.github.io/assets/files/keir_1.pdf#:~:text=,significantly%20better%20than%20a). They showed modest gains but not surpassing other PRF techniques.
- **LEE - Latent Entity Expansion (Macdonald et al., 2023):** This recent method achieved _SOTA first-stage results_. **LEE** performs an **iterative expansion**: it alternates between reranking with a neural model and expanding the query with both important terms _and entities_ from top documents[\[40\]](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.17082v2#:~:text=Figure%C2%A02%20depicts%20our%20Latent%20Entity,final%20set%20of%20documents%2C)[\[41\]](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.17082v2#:~:text=). By updating the query after each batch of documents, it mimics a researcher digging deeper. On Robust04, LEE (with BERT reranker in the loop) produced outstanding recall (best R@1000) and the **highest first-pass MAP reported**[\[42\]](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.17082v2#:~:text=substantially%20improving%20or%20not%20significantly,combination%20with%20NLM%20passage%20ranking)[\[43\]](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.17082v2#:~:text=improvements%2C%20LEE%20with%20NLM%20would,PRF%2C%20and%20RM3). In a single re-rank pipeline, _LEE outperformed prior expansion methods like CEQE, ColBERT-PRF, and RM3_[\[43\]](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.17082v2#:~:text=improvements%2C%20LEE%20with%20NLM%20would,PRF%2C%20and%20RM3). With two rerank passes (initial BM25, mid LEE expansion, final BERT), it reached **MAP 0.393 on title queries and 0.438 on description queries**[\[3\]](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.17082v2#:~:text=NDCG%20MAP%20R%401000%20NDCG%20MAP,377) - a new state-of-the-art. LEE's strength comes from mixing lexical (word) and semantic (entity) signals adaptively. _Key techniques:_ probabilistic selection of both words and entity links as expansion terms, with a duet of term-based and entity-based retrieval scores[\[40\]](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.17082v2#:~:text=Figure%C2%A02%20depicts%20our%20Latent%20Entity,final%20set%20of%20documents%2C)[\[44\]](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.17082v2#:~:text=The%20final%20score%20of%20a,based%20query%20expansion%20retrieval%20scores). Entities (e.g. "World Court" -> expansion with "International Court of Justice") provide context that plain terms might miss.
- **Modular Reranking (MORES, 2022):** Gao & Callan's **MORES+** framework can be seen as a sophisticated multi-stage approach in one model. They **split long documents into passages, encode them, then allow the query to attend to _all passages at once_**[\[45\]](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2205.04275#:~:text=bottleneck%3A%20the%20low%20dimension%20representations,Our%20code%20is%20available%20at)[\[46\]](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2205.04275#:~:text=documents%20beyond%20this%20length%20has,query%20to%20document%20cross%20attention). This is effectively a _form of pseudo-feedback_: the model can draw information from across the document (like multiple pieces of evidence). MORES+ achieved **SOTA on Robust04 description queries (NDCG and MAP)**[\[47\]](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2205.04275#:~:text=decent%20utility,will%20have%20further%20future%20impacts)[\[48\]](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2205.04275#:~:text=Our%20experiments%20demonstrate%20the%20effectiveness,and%20best%20Dev%20set%20performance). While it's a single model, it conceptually replaces a pipeline of "BM25→passage rankers→full doc aggregation" with one Transformer that does full-document cross-attention. The result was **better use of long documents** - important for Robust04's news articles, which can be lengthy. MORES+ notably _outperformed prior long-document methods like PARADE (which did passage-score pooling)_, especially on natural language queries[\[18\]](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2205.04275#:~:text=On%20the%20other%20hand%2C%20when,will%20have%20further%20future%20impacts)[\[49\]](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2205.04275#:~:text=We%20also%20see%20that%20MORES%2B,rankers%20support%20document). In robust04's title queries, MORES+ was on par with the best (since titles are short)[\[15\]](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2205.04275#:~:text=On%20Title%20queries%2C%20many%20neural,compared%20with%20MORES%2B%20entries%2C%20the), but on descriptions it led by a clear margin[\[18\]](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2205.04275#:~:text=On%20the%20other%20hand%2C%20when,will%20have%20further%20future%20impacts). _Techniques:_ "modular" design - separate encoding of passages, then a joint Transformer for interaction; cross-attention that lets the query attend to any part of any passage[\[46\]](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2205.04275#:~:text=documents%20beyond%20this%20length%20has,query%20to%20document%20cross%20attention); plus use of a _dense retriever_ (coCondenser) for initial candidate recall[\[25\]](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2205.04275#:~:text=MS,493).
- **Multidimensional Relevance (Pan et al., 2024):** A recent approach is to use **sentence-level and passage-level semantic signals** for expansion[\[50\]](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-82871-0?error=cookies_not_supported&code=39586133-7e4d-4b78-a9a5-0fb6cd5f5a6b#:~:text=information%20retrieval%2C%20particularly%20for%20improving,more%20possibilities%20for%20query%20expansion)[\[51\]](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-82871-0?error=cookies_not_supported&code=39586133-7e4d-4b78-a9a5-0fb6cd5f5a6b#:~:text=statistical%20information.%20Then%2C%20the%20pre,effectively%20harnessed%20to%20enhance%20retrieval). Pan et al. propose extracting relevant sentences and passages via a pre-trained model and using those to weight expansion terms. Their _multi-dimensional semantic PRF_ improved over baseline BM25 on Robust04[\[52\]](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-82871-0?error=cookies_not_supported&code=39586133-7e4d-4b78-a9a5-0fb6cd5f5a6b#:~:text=for%20the%20second%20retrieval%20round,effectively%20harnessed%20to%20enhance%20retrieval) and was competitive with some neural models. However, they observed that an earlier CEQE method still had an edge on MAP[\[7\]](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-82871-0?error=cookies_not_supported&code=39586133-7e4d-4b78-a9a5-0fb6cd5f5a6b#:~:text=match%20at%20L873%20advanced%20models,to%20the%20CEQE%20model%E2%80%99s%20effective). This illustrates that carefully incorporating semantic info from feedback is challenging, and the best methods (like LEE or MORES) explicitly integrate with rerankers or full interactions.

Finally, **hybrid fusion** of sparse and dense results often yields strong performance. A simple yet effective strategy is to **merge BM25 and dense retriever scores**. This can be done by linear combination or rank fusion. Many BEIR benchmark submissions use this, as dense and sparse often retrieve complementary items. On Robust04, a hybrid of BM25 and a dense model can outperform either alone - ensuring both exact keywords and semantic matches are covered. For example, Vespa's team showed that a dense retriever fine-tuned on MSMARCO, when combined with BM25 in a hybrid zero-shot setting, improved nDCG on Robust04 compared to using the dense model alone (which had underperformed BM25 in that domain)[\[13\]](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0306457322001364#:~:text=expansion%20www,the%20Robust04%20and%20WT10G). In general, **sparse-dense fusion** is a practical way to get the best of both worlds.

## Conclusion

Robust04 remains a challenging testbed, but the past few years have seen **MAP scores leap from ~0.33 (pre-BERT era) to ~0. Forty+**. The _current leader_ is around **MAP 0.43-0.45** on description queries (LEE and MORES+ methods with multi-stage reranking) and around **0.39-0.40** on title queries[\[3\]](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.17082v2#:~:text=NDCG%20MAP%20R%401000%20NDCG%20MAP,377)[\[4\]](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2205.04275#:~:text=Table%204%20shows%20the%20performance,art%20in%20ranking%20effectiveness). Key trends include:

- **Neural rerankers** (BERT, T5, etc.) deliver huge gains by deep semantic matching[\[9\]](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.06713#:~:text=our%20model%20with%20CEDR%20,3B%20model%20produces%20the).
- **Large models & LLMs** (T5-3B, GPT-4) further push effectiveness, even zero-shot, by leveraging vast pre-trained knowledge[\[2\]](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.06713#:~:text=%2B%20T5,to%20run%20these%20experiments%20due)[\[32\]](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-emnlp.305.pdf#:~:text=FLAN,26).
- **Feedback and expansion** (RM3, CEQE, ColBERT-PRF, LEE) remain critical for difficult collections - modern techniques intelligently use neural signals to expand queries or aggregate evidence[\[43\]](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.17082v2#:~:text=improvements%2C%20LEE%20with%20NLM%20would,PRF%2C%20and%20RM3)[\[8\]](https://discovery.researcher.life/topic/pseudo-relevance-feedback-technique/24929746?page=1&topic_name=Pseudo%20Relevance%20Feedback%20Technique#:~:text=Pseudo%20Relevance%20Feedback%20Technique%20Research,on%20the%20MSMARCO%20document).
- **Hybrid approaches** (combining lexical and semantic) consistently perform well, as they cover each other's weaknesses[\[13\]](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0306457322001364#:~:text=expansion%20www,the%20Robust04%20and%20WT10G).
- **Efficiency techniques** (distillation, late interaction, modular design) are enabling these advanced models to be used in practice on large corpora[\[23\]](https://aclanthology.org/anthology-files/pdf/D/D19/D19-3004.pdf#:~:text=Researchers%20have%20developed%20a%20panoply,which%20in%02tegrates%20the%20Anserini%20information)[\[49\]](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2205.04275#:~:text=We%20also%20see%20that%20MORES%2B,rankers%20support%20document).

Robust04 has thus been a driving force for **information retrieval innovation** - from BM25 to neural hybrids. As of 2025, the frontier is moving toward using **large generative models and advanced feedback loops** to squeeze out further improvements, potentially breaking the 0.50 MAP barrier on this dataset in the near future. Each new technique not only sets a new high score but also offers insights into how to better understand and retrieve text at scale.
